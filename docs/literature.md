# Mapping the question onto existing work

This note is a short map, not a literature review. Citations below were checked against publisher pages, author PDFs, or arXiv abstracts. No page numbers or numerical results are quoted unless they appear in those public abstracts or title pages. If a claim could not be verified, that is stated.

The research question here is evolutionary: when an agent is a **controller inside a harness** (tools, sensors, memory, actuators), does selection first reshape the harness, the way limbs and organs were under selection before nervous systems could redesign them, or can the controller usefully decide which harness parts to keep?

## Morphological computation (body as the first computer)

Pfeifer and colleagues treat “embodiment” as more than “intelligence needs a body.” **Morphological computation** is the claim that shape, materials, sensors, and the body–environment coupling take on work that a controller would otherwise have to do.

- Rolf Pfeifer and Fumiya Iida, “Morphological computation: connecting body, brain, and environment,” *Journal of the Robotics Society of Japan* (2005). Author PDF: the paper argues that not everything needs to be controlled by the brain; morphology, materials, and interaction can take over or distribute control (their examples include passive stabilization and adaptive grasping). [https://people.csail.mit.edu/iida/papers/pfeifer_iida_JSM05.pdf](https://people.csail.mit.edu/iida/papers/pfeifer_iida_JSM05.pdf)
- Rolf Pfeifer, Fumiya Iida, and Gabriel Gómez, “Morphological computation for adaptive behavior and cognition,” *International Congress Series* 1291 (2006): 22–29, doi:10.1016/j.ics.2005.12.080. The abstract frames morphological computation as a **task distribution** among neural controller, morphology (shape, sensors, actuators, materials), and environment.

Rolf Pfeifer and Josh Bongard, *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think: A New View of Intelligence* (MIT Press / Bradford, 2006). MIT Press lists the book as arguing that thought is constrained and enabled by morphology and material properties, using “understanding by building.” This repo does **not** quote chapter-level results from the book; only the publisher description was checked. [https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262537421/how-the-body-shapes-the-way-we-think/](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262537421/how-the-body-shapes-the-way-we-think/)

**Map onto this sim:** a low-capacity controller is a primitive nervous system. If a cheaper generalist tool or a well-chosen specialist set can raise expected success without a richer policy table, that is morphological computation in miniature. Hypothesis H1 is the operational version.

## Coevolving body and brain

### Sims 1994 — virtual creatures

Karl Sims, “Evolving Virtual Creatures,” in *SIGGRAPH ’94* (24 July 1994), ACM doi:10.1145/192161.192167. Author PDF: [https://www.karlsims.com/papers/siggraph94.pdf](https://www.karlsims.com/papers/siggraph94.pdf). A CUNY teaching copy of the same PDF lists the printed pagination as *Computer Graphics*, Annual Conference Series, July 1994, pp. 15–22.

Verified from the ACM abstract and the PDF’s opening: both **morphology** and the **neural system that controls muscle forces** are generated by a genetic algorithm; directed graphs are the genotype for body and nervous system; fitness functions target swimming, walking, jumping, and following. Complexity (adding or removing features) can itself evolve. This repo does not re-state Sims’s qualitative creature gallery as quantitative findings.

**Map:** Sims’s default is *coevolution* (regime 3 here), not “brain redesigns the body.” Morphology is under the same blind variation-and-selection as the controller.

### Lipson & Pollack 2000 — GOLEM

Hod Lipson and Jordan B. Pollack, “Automatic design and manufacture of robotic lifeforms,” *Nature* **406**, 974–978 (2000), doi:10.1038/35023115. [https://www.nature.com/articles/35023115](https://www.nature.com/articles/35023115)

Verified from the Nature abstract and the authors’ GOLEM project page: bars, actuators, and artificial neurons evolve in simulation for locomotion distance; machines are then fabricated. The project page states that body and brain were **co-evolved simultaneously**, starting from empty individuals. Specific generation counts and selection-operator probabilities appear in the paper PDF; this map does not rely on those details.

### Bongard — artificial ontogeny

Josh C. Bongard and Rolf Pfeifer, “Repeated Structure and Dissociation of Genotypic and Phenotypic Complexity in Artificial Ontogeny,” GECCO 2001. Author PDF: [https://meclab.w3.uvm.edu/papers/2001_GECCO_Bongard.pdf](https://meclab.w3.uvm.edu/papers/2001_GECCO_Bongard.pdf). The paper evolves morphology and neural control of block-pushing agents via a developmental (gene-regulation) mapping, and cites Sims (1994) among prior complete-agent encodings.

Josh C. Bongard, “Evolving Complete Agents using Artificial Ontogeny,” 2003. Author PDF: [https://roderik.net/bongard2003.pdf](https://roderik.net/bongard2003.pdf). Abstract: genotypes are treated as genetic regulatory networks that grow both morphology and neural control; tasks include directed locomotion and block pushing in noise.

**Map:** development is another way the harness can change without a smart brain choosing the edit. This sim uses a much flatter encoding (bit-vector tools, prototype slots).

### Nolfi & Floreano — evolutionary robotics

Stefano Nolfi and Dario Floreano, *Evolutionary Robotics: The Biology, Intelligence, and Technology of Self-Organizing Machines* (MIT Press / Bradford, 2000). MIT Press describes populations of controllers evaluated in an environment, with selection and mutation, including a chapter titled “Competitive Co-Evolution.” This repo did **not** re-read the book; chapter-level empirical claims are not repeated here. [https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262640565/evolutionary-robotics/](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262640565/evolutionary-robotics/)

Paolo Pagliuca and Stefano Nolfi, “The Dynamic of Body and Brain Co-Evolution,” arXiv:2011.11440 (23 Nov 2020). The abstract (checked on the preprint) says robots with co-adapted body and control traits outperform robots with fixed hand-designed morphologies, and that the reported advantage is **mutual scaffolding** rather than simply “selecting better shapes.” Morphological variation is argued not to be necessarily destructive. Treat this as a **preprint abstract**, not a journal-of-record result.

**Map:** H4 (coevolution can win, and can also churn) is the cheap analogue of “mutual scaffolding vs destructive morphological mutation.”

## Brooks — intelligence in the sensor–motor loop

Rodney A. Brooks, “A Robust Layered Control System for a Mobile Robot,” *IEEE Journal of Robotics and Automation* 2(1): 14–23 (March 1986), doi:10.1109/JRA.1986.1087032. This is the original **subsumption** paper: layers of competence, asynchronous modules, higher layers suppress lower outputs while lower layers keep running.

Rodney A. Brooks, “Intelligence without representation,” *Artificial Intelligence* 47 (1991): 139–159, doi:10.1016/0004-3702(91)90053-M. Author PDF: [https://people.csail.mit.edu/brooks/papers/representation.pdf](https://people.csail.mit.edu/brooks/papers/representation.pdf). Abstract: incremental creatures that interface to the world through perception and action; decomposition into parallel activity producers rather than a central representational bottleneck.

**Map:** subsumption is a *designed* harness of behaviors, not an evolved tool catalog and not a model that edits its own sensors. It is the philosophical opposite of “the LLM chooses which tools to keep.” The sim’s low-capacity regime is closer to Brooks’s bet (a thin controller plus the right wiring) than to a frontier LLM.

## LLM agents: the brain tries to run the harness

These papers are about **using** or **writing** tools, not about population genetics of tool genomes. They are the “brain-directed harness edit” story in the wild.

### Toolformer

Timo Schick, Jane Dwivedi-Yu, Roberto Dessì, Roberta Raileanu, Maria Lomeli, Eric Hambro, Luke Zettlemoyer, Nicola Cancedda, and Thomas Scialom, “Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools,” NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2302.04761. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)

Verified from the abstract: a language model is trained (self-supervised) to decide **which APIs to call, when, with what arguments**, and how to insert the result. Tools in the paper include a calculator, Q&A, search, translation, and a calendar. Filtering of sampled API calls uses whether they reduce future-token loss; the model is then finetuned on the helpful calls. The tool *set* is given; the model learns a policy over it.

**Map:** Toolformer is brain-directed **tool use**, not brain-directed **tool evolution**. Regime 4 in this sim is harsher: the value estimate is allowed to *mutate or drop* a limb, not only choose when to swing it.

### Voyager skill libraries

Guanzhi Wang, Yuqi Xie, Yunfan Jiang, Ajay Mandlekar, Chaowei Xiao, Yuke Zhu, Linxi Fan, and Anima Anandkumar, “Voyager: An Open-Ended Embodied Agent with Large Language Models,” arXiv:2305.16291 (2023). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291) Project page: [https://voyager.minedojo.org/](https://voyager.minedojo.org/)

Verified from the abstract and project page: three named components are an automatic curriculum, an ever-growing **skill library of executable code**, and iterative prompting with environment feedback, execution errors, and self-verification. The agent queries GPT-4 as a black box (no weight updates). The abstract reports 3.3× more unique items, 2.3× longer travel, and up to 15.3× faster tech-tree milestones versus prior SOTA **in their Minecraft comparison**. Those numbers are the authors’ reported results, not replicated here.

**Map:** Voyager is the closest LLM analogue of a brain that *grows* a harness (skills as limbs). It is still myopic in the sense that skills are added when they look locally useful. Hypothesis H3 asks whether that kind of local value estimate can discard a delayed-reward tool that blind selection would keep.

## What this repo is not claiming

- It does not simulate a language model, a Minecraft agent, or a 3D creature.
- It does not test whether Toolformer’s or Voyager’s published numbers are right.
- It does not settle whether real agent products should freeze the model and evolve tools, or the reverse. It only asks whether, in a capacity-limited evolutionary toy, the **order of adaptation** (harness vs controller vs a greedy editor) changes fitness on three synthetic tasks.

Unverified on purpose (do not treat as citations from this note): later “LLM tool-use” surveys, un-checked evolutionary-robotics papers beyond those listed, and any secondary blog summary of Sims or Brooks.
